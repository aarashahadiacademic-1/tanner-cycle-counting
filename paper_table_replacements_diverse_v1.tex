\subsection{Benchmark Construction}
We describe the Tanner graphs used in
Tables~\ref{tab:exact-exp} and~\ref{tab:hybrid-exp}.
Small degrees reduce the alternating branching factors in $\omega$,
motivating small-degree benchmarks. These are synthetic sparse
parity-check matrices, not standardized codes or decoding-performance tests.

Bases A--C use point--line incidence $y=sx+b$ over $\mathbb F_Q$,
the finite field with $Q$ elements, with
$x\in\{0,\ldots,d_c-1\}$ and $s\in\{0,\ldots,d_v-1\}$.
Their parameters $(Q,d_v,d_c)$ are $(7,3,6)$, $(5,3,4)$, and
$(11,4,8)$, respectively. This gives the stated degrees and excludes
$4$-cycles. Base D is obtained from the symplectic generalized quadrangle
 over $\mathbb F_5$ by splitting each degree-six point into two
 degree-three variables; it has 312 variables, 156 checks, and girth eight.
Labels A--D specify the base, lift size, and cyclic ($c$) or random ($r$) lift.

R1200 and R2400 are constructed by random socket pairing, rejecting
parallel edges and removing $4$-cycles by degree-preserving switches.
P1200 and P1600 use our degree-constrained variant of progressive
edge growth (PEG)~\cite{hu2005}: each new edge selects a check of maximum
current distance with remaining capacity, breaking ties by minimum degree
and then randomly. Construction restarts if the degree constraints
cannot be completed without $4$-cycles. The R/P label gives the number
of variable nodes. Thus the benchmarks span four construction families.
Generators, fixed seeds, and realized edge lists are in the repository
linked above. Every graph was checked for simplicity, connectivity,
degrees, and girth; exact girth-cycle counts agree with independent
exhaustive NB enumeration.

Table~\ref{tab:hybrid-exp} uses $r=100$ and $K=10n$.
Zero MARE results from exact fallback in every run.
The random-pairing and PEG instances also trigger exact fallback,
showing that their high girth or non-algebraic construction alone does
not favor sampling. Matched cyclic/random lifts retain the contrast
between cycle-rich and closure-sparse regimes. The results support
hybrid estimation at high normalized closure abundance on the tested
graphs, without implying superiority across LDPC families.
