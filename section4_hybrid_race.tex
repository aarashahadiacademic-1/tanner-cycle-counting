The exact routine and inverse-binomial sampler run concurrently on
separate C++ threads. The first completion determines the output:
the exact count or the estimate from the $r$th closure. The losing
worker receives a cancellation signal; exact search checks it during
root processing and DFS. Measured timings are first-winner latencies;
joining the losing thread is excluded. Independent seeds are used in
100 repetitions. The target $r=101$ follows from the MARE bound at
$\varepsilon=0.08$; no pilot or sampling cap is used.

Table~\ref{tab:hybrid-exp} illustrates both outcomes. Cycle-rich
C128c, A256c, and D16c attain speedups of $13.03\times$,
$4.93\times$, and $4.41\times$, whereas sparse A128r, A256r,
and B128r always return exact answers but incur concurrency overhead.
On small instances, thread startup and scheduling can dominate.
Observed MARE can exceed $\varepsilon$ in a finite batch: the bound
concerns expected error, not every empirical sample average.
These measurements do not establish uniform wall-clock dominance.
